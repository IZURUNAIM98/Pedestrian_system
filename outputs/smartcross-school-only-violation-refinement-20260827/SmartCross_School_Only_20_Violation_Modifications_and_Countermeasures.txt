SMARTCROSS
SCHOOL-ONLY REFINEMENT OF THE EXISTING 20 VIOLATION CASES
AND THEIR PEDESTRIAN-SAFETY COUNTERMEASURES

Purpose
-------
Keep the existing Normal Crossing scenario unchanged. Apply only small,
school-specific modifications to the existing 20 cases when the user selects
the School Crossing scenario.

Scope rules
-----------
1. Do not replace the existing 20-case catalogue.
2. Do not apply the school-zone treatment to the Normal Crossing scenario.
3. Retain the existing webpage layout, controls and general seven-stage
   simulation sequence.
4. Change only the school context, affected pedestrian, actor behaviour,
   warning text, detection response and final countermeasure where necessary.
5. A pupil may enter the crossing only when WALK is displayed and conflicting
   vehicles have stopped at their marked stopping points.
6. Where both Vehicle A and Vehicle B conflict with the pupil movement, both
   vehicles must stop before the crossing before WALK is released.
7. Collision and fire cases are retained because they are part of the current
   20-case catalogue, but they should be described as emergency outcomes rather
   than ordinary driving offences.


01. CROSSING-ZONE SPEEDING
Existing case:
Vehicle approaches the crossing above the configured safe speed.

School-only modification:
Present the event as speeding while the school-zone control is active during
pupil arrival or departure. Use a pupil or pupil group as the affected road
user. The school-zone markings and warnings appear only in the School Crossing
scenario.

School pedestrian countermeasure:
Detect the excessive approach speed early, display a SCHOOL-ZONE SPEED alert,
keep the pupil signal at WAIT, extend the vehicle stopping phase and release
WALK only after the approaching vehicle has stopped safely.


02. RED-SIGNAL VIOLATION
Existing case:
Vehicle crosses the stop line while its traffic signal is RED.

School-only modification:
Link the RED phase to an active pupil crossing request. Show pupils waiting at
the school gate or crossing entrance before the vehicle breaches the signal.

School pedestrian countermeasure:
Keep the pupil signal at WAIT, sound a priority warning, highlight the violating
vehicle and hold all pupil movement until the conflict area is clear and the
controller has restored a safe protected phase.


03. FAILURE TO YIELD
Existing case:
Vehicle does not yield to a pedestrian using the zebra crossing.

School-only modification:
Use a pupil group, optionally accompanied by a guardian or simulated crossing
warden. The vehicle must approach visibly before failing to yield.

School pedestrian countermeasure:
Detect the pupil group, command both conflicting vehicles to stop near their
marked stopping points and release WALK only after both vehicles are stationary.
If either vehicle continues, retain WAIT and issue a failure-to-yield alert.


04. VEHICLE STOPPED ON THE ZEBRA CROSSING
Existing case:
Vehicle enters the zebra crossing and stops on it.

School-only modification:
Show the vehicle blocking the direct pupil route between the school gate and
the opposite footway. Once stopped on the crossing, the vehicle must remain in
that blocked position and must not reverse unexpectedly.

School pedestrian countermeasure:
Withhold WALK, mark the zebra crossing as obstructed and direct pupils to remain
inside the safe waiting area. Release the crossing only after the obstruction
has been removed and the crossing is confirmed clear.


05. ACCESSIBILITY OBSTRUCTION
Existing case:
Vehicle blocks the kerb ramp, tactile paving or accessible crossing route.

School-only modification:
Use a pupil with a mobility aid or visual impairment, accompanied where
appropriate by a guardian. The obstruction should affect the route from the
school gate to the crossing.

School pedestrian countermeasure:
Detect the blocked accessible path, display ACCESSIBLE ROUTE BLOCKED, prevent
WALK from starting and require the vehicle to clear the kerb ramp and tactile
route before the pupil movement is authorised.


06. MOTORCYCLE FILTERING
Existing case:
Motorcycle filters past stopped or slow vehicles near the crossing.

School-only modification:
Place the motorcycle beside a school drop-off queue. The motorcycle should
continue forward towards and across the stop line; it must not reverse before
reaching the crossing.

School pedestrian countermeasure:
Use side-approach detection to identify the motorcycle, keep pupils at WAIT and
delay WALK until the motorcycle has stopped or fully cleared the conflict area.
Display MOTORCYCLE FILTERING IN SCHOOL ZONE.


07. PREMATURE PEDESTRIAN MOVEMENT
Existing case:
Pedestrian attempts to cross before WALK is displayed.

School-only modification:
Use a pupil who moves towards the crossing edge but remains inside the protected
waiting area. The pupil must not walk onto the zebra crossing while WAIT or STOP
is displayed.

School pedestrian countermeasure:
Apply a strict signal interlock: pedestrian motion begins only during WALK and
only after Vehicle A and Vehicle B have stopped where both are conflicting.
Provide an audible or visual WAIT reminder suitable for school users.


08. UNSAFE OVERTAKING
Existing case:
One vehicle overtakes another vehicle that has stopped for the crossing.

School-only modification:
Vehicle A yields to pupils while Vehicle B overtakes beside the school crossing
or drop-off queue. Show only vehicle motion; no unnecessary red dotted path is
required.

School pedestrian countermeasure:
Detect the overtaking trajectory, retain the pupil WAIT indication, issue an
OVERTAKING AT SCHOOL CROSSING alert and allow pupils to move only after Vehicle B
has stopped or cleared the crossing.


09. WRONG-WAY APPROACH
Existing case:
Vehicle approaches the crossing against the intended traffic direction.

School-only modification:
Present the event as wrong-way travel within the school arrival or departure
circulation route.

School pedestrian countermeasure:
Trigger a wrong-way warning as soon as the direction conflict is detected,
freeze the pupil crossing request at WAIT and stop all conflicting vehicle
movements before a protected pupil phase can begin.


10. OPPOSING-LANE BYPASS
Existing case:
Vehicle uses the opposing lane to pass a queue near the crossing.

School-only modification:
The queue represents school arrival or collection traffic, and the bypassing
vehicle approaches a pupil group screened by the queue.

School pedestrian countermeasure:
Monitor the opposing lane, warn pupils and the controller, keep WALK withheld
and extend the clearance period until the bypassing vehicle has stopped or left
the school crossing conflict zone.


11. UNSAFE LANE CHANGE
Existing case:
Vehicle changes lane inside the crossing approach zone.

School-only modification:
The lane change occurs beside the school drop-off area and reduces visibility
between the driver and pupils. Show the vehicle movement without an unnecessary
red dotted path.

School pedestrian countermeasure:
Detect the lateral movement, cancel or delay the pending WALK phase and display
UNSAFE LANE CHANGE - PUPILS WAIT. Restart the safe crossing sequence only after
the vehicle is stable and the approach is clear.


12. TAILGATING
Existing case:
A following vehicle travels too closely behind a vehicle yielding at the
crossing.

School-only modification:
Vehicle A stops for pupils while Vehicle B closes the gap in the school-zone
approach.

School pedestrian countermeasure:
Measure the unsafe following gap, hold pupils at WAIT and require Vehicle B to
stop behind Vehicle A. WALK is released only when both vehicles are stationary
and sufficient separation from the crossing is confirmed.


13. DISTRACTED OR DELAYED DRIVER RESPONSE
Existing case:
Driver responds late to a pedestrian crossing demand.

School-only modification:
Show a delayed response while pupils are visible at the school crossing. The
simulation should describe the observable late response without claiming to
identify a specific private activity inside the vehicle.

School pedestrian countermeasure:
Issue an early attention alert, keep pupils at WAIT and extend the vehicle
approach monitoring period. Begin WALK only after the vehicle has completed a
safe stop.


14. EMERGENCY BRAKING
Existing case:
Vehicle approaches, brakes sharply and stops near the crossing.

School-only modification:
Show Vehicle A in the approach stage first, followed by hazard detection,
emergency braking and a complete stop before the school crossing line.

School pedestrian countermeasure:
Keep pupils at WAIT throughout the approach and braking stages. Confirm zero
vehicle speed and a safe stopping position before changing the pupil signal to
WALK. If the vehicle cannot stop safely, retain WAIT until the conflict clears.


15. U-TURN AT THE CROSSING
Existing case:
Vehicle performs a U-turn within the crossing approach.

School-only modification:
Place the U-turn immediately outside the school gate or drop-off area. Vehicle A
must follow a slow, smooth curved motion along the intended path so the sequence
is easy to understand.

School pedestrian countermeasure:
Detect the turning trajectory, prevent pupils from entering the crossing and
display U-TURN AT SCHOOL GATE. Release WALK only after the vehicle has completed
the turn and left the pupil conflict area.


16. VULNERABLE PEDESTRIAN CONFLICT
Existing case:
Vehicle creates a conflict with a slower or vulnerable pedestrian.

School-only modification:
Use a young child, a pupil with a disability or a pupil group requiring a longer
clearance period. If emergency braking occurs, the vehicle must stop before the
crossing line and before entering the pupil path.

School pedestrian countermeasure:
Apply child-sensitive detection, a longer WALK and clearance period, and a
vehicle hold until the pupil has completely reached the safe footway. Do not
release conflicting traffic early.


17. SCHOOL-ZONE VIOLATION
Existing case:
Vehicle disregards an active school-zone rule or control.

School-only modification:
Retain this case only in the School Crossing scenario. School-zone lines,
frontage markings, gate controls and school-specific warnings must not appear in
the Normal Crossing scenario.

School pedestrian countermeasure:
Activate the school-zone warning state, enforce the configured school operating
rule, keep pupils at WAIT during any breach and record the event as a
school-zone-specific violation.


18. PARKING-RELATED OBSTRUCTION
Existing case:
Parked vehicle blocks visibility or access near the crossing.

School-only modification:
Place the vehicle near the school gate, drop-off area or zebra crossing so it
conceals a child from approaching drivers or blocks the pupil waiting area.

School pedestrian countermeasure:
Detect the occupied keep-clear or visibility zone, display SCHOOL CROSSING VIEW
OBSTRUCTED and withhold WALK until the sight line and pupil route are clear.


19. COLLISION EVENT
Existing case:
Vehicle collides with a road user or crossing infrastructure.

School-only modification:
For a pupil collision, both the pupil and vehicle must move to the zebra-crossing
conflict point before the alarm and red impact marker appear. For an
infrastructure collision, Vehicle A follows the defined path and contacts the
traffic-light pole before the impact indication appears.

School pedestrian countermeasure:
Immediately set all vehicle and pupil signals to STOP, sound the emergency
alarm, prevent further actor movement, identify the impact location and begin
the simulated school emergency-response sequence. The impact marker must never
appear before contact.


20. VEHICLE FIRE EVENT
Existing case:
Vehicle develops smoke, fire or an explosion near the crossing.

School-only modification:
Locate the event near the school frontage while pupils remain within a safe
waiting or evacuation area. Treat this as an emergency outcome, not an ordinary
moving violation.

School pedestrian countermeasure:
Set all crossing indications to STOP, prevent pupils from approaching the
affected vehicle, display a clear FIRE / EVACUATION alert and begin the simulated
emergency notification and safe-area sequence.


RECOMMENDED IMPLEMENTATION APPROACH
-----------------------------------
1. Keep the current 20 identifiers and selection order so existing controls and
   references remain familiar.
2. Keep the Normal Crossing definitions and motion profiles unchanged.
3. Add a School-specific configuration layer for:
   - pupil or pupil-group actors;
   - school-gate, drop-off and school-zone context;
   - school-only warning messages;
   - longer child clearance timing where required;
   - vehicle-stop confirmation before WALK;
   - collision and emergency response wording.
4. Reuse common motion only where it remains technically appropriate, but apply
   the school-specific trigger, stopping rule and pedestrian countermeasure.
5. Validate every School case against these safety invariants:
   - no pupil enters during WAIT or STOP;
   - no WALK while a conflicting vehicle is moving;
   - both conflicting vehicles stop when both affect the pupil movement;
   - impact alerts occur only after simulated contact;
   - School-zone graphics never appear in Normal Crossing;
   - every completed vehicle stop reports zero speed.

DECISION SUMMARY
----------------
Recommended decision: retain the existing Normal Crossing catalogue and apply
the above limited modifications only to the School Crossing version of the same
20 cases. This provides a clear school-pedestrian safety distinction without a
complete replacement of both scenario catalogues.
